# Student AI Use Guide

A resource faculty share with students to guide how they use AI in their coursework. Students use it at three moments: before they ask AI, before they use AI's answer, and before they turn in their work.

**View the guide:** https://octc-id.github.io/student-ai-use-guide/

## What this is

The guide coaches students through AI use without teaching them a framework. It gives students one routine, **the 3 Qs**, and the same words their instructors use in the syllabus, assignment directions, and feedback.

| Moment | The question |
|---|---|
| **Before you ask AI** | Should I use AI for this part of my assignment? |
| **Before you use AI's answer** | How would I know if AI's answer is wrong? |
| **Before you turn in your work** | Can I explain my work and how AI helped me? |

Before the 3 Qs, students check the assignment's **AI use label**: No AI, AI-supported, or AI-integrated. Instructors set the label for each assignment.

The guide never makes the rules. Every page sends students to their instructor's **AI Course Policy** and the assignment directions. The rules stay with each instructor.

The guide is the student-facing companion to the [AI Literacy Faculty Playbook and BE the Change Workbook](https://github.com/OCTC-ID/AI-literacy-guidebook-4264). The 3 Qs line up with the BE behaviors underneath, but students don't need to learn the framework to use them.

## How faculty use it

The home page is built to sit in a course's **Start Here** module in Blackboard Ultra, next to the instructor's own AI Course Policy. Students can read it in under a minute. Each "More on this" link opens the matching page in a new tab.

Faculty can also link to a single page from assignment directions. For example: "Before you use any AI output in this section, see *Before You Use AI's Answer*."

Link to the live site or embed it instead of copying the text into a course. That way every course shows the current wording.

## Shared language

The guide uses these terms word for word. Please don't reword them in pages you add.

- **AI use label**: No AI, AI-supported, or AI-integrated. Never "level," which belongs to the OCTC framework's Level 1–3.
- **The 3 Qs** and the three **moments**, in the exact wording above.
- **Keep a trail**: Word saved to OneDrive, a OneNote notebook for each course, and a written record of AI use.
- **AI Course Policy** and **Academic Integrity**: the Simple Syllabus labels.

## Notes for editing

- **Brand colors.** Colors and the font match the faculty playbook: OCTC blue `#00467f`, dark blue `#00345f`, navy `#011d41`, gold `#e7a614`, and the Aptos font stack. Gold is used only on navy or as a line or border, never as text on white, for contrast.
- **Upload files together.** When a page and `styles.css` change at the same time, upload both.
- **Version tag.** Pages load `css/styles.css?v=3`. After changing the stylesheet, raise the number on every page so browsers load the new version.
- **Home page links open in a new tab.** The home page is often shown inside Blackboard, so its links open a new tab to keep students from getting lost in the frame. Pages on the site itself open in the same tab.
- **Plain language.** Write for about a 6th–8th grade reading level. Name the thing instead of using "it" or "this." Avoid "X, not Y" contrasts and filler words like "crucial" and "leverage."
- **Accessibility.** Keep the viewport tag, the skip link, and the `visually-hidden` notes on links that open a new tab. Meaning never depends on color alone.

## Adapting this for your campus

The guide is written to be campus-neutral. It refers to Microsoft tools (Word, OneDrive, OneNote, Copilot) because OCTC students have them through their college accounts. If your students use different tools, update the Keep a Trail section. Also check that the AI use labels match what your faculty use.
