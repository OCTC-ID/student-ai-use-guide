2 Before you use it

How would I know if AI's answer is wrong?

Check AI's answer before you copy it, study from it, or use it in your work.

Why it matters

AI chat tools write by guessing which words usually come next. They do not look up facts the way your textbook or a library database does. So an answer can sound sure of itself and still be wrong.

AI can:

  • Make up facts, sources, or quotes that look real.
  • Give information that is out of date.
  • Be right in general but wrong for your course, your field, or your assignment.
  • Answer a different question than the one you asked.

How to check AI's answer

  • Look it up somewhere else

    Find the main facts in your textbook, your course materials, or another source you trust. If AI gave you a source, open it. Make sure the source says what AI says it does.

  • Compare it to the assignment

    Read the assignment directions again. Does AI's answer do what your instructor asked?

  • Explain it in your own words

    If you can't explain AI's answer, you can't tell yet if it is right.

  • Ask AI what it is unsure about

    AI's reply can show you where to look more closely.

    This does not prove anything. AI can be wrong about this too. Always use at least one of the other checks as well.

If you can't check part of AI's answer

You can leave that part out. You can say in your work that you could not confirm it. Or you can ask your instructor, a tutor, or a librarian for help.

In clinical, lab, and shop settings, always check anything about procedures or safety against your program's materials and with your instructor.

Examples

A source that didn't exist

A student asks AI for articles to use in a paper. AI gives her three. She searches for them in the library database. One is real. The other two do not exist. She keeps the real one and asks a librarian to help her find two more.

Settings for the shop

A student asks AI what machine settings to use for a lab project. Before he tries them, he compares AI's answer to the equipment chart and his program's procedure sheet. They do not match. He follows the procedure sheet and asks his instructor about the difference.
